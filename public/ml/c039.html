thieves emptied a large part of the vaults of the Antwerp Diamond Centre, in Antwerp, Belgium. The moment had been chosen. All week the city was following a tennis tournament, the Proximus Diamond Games, which Venus Williams won on Sunday the 16th. It was also the day after Valentine’s Day. And it was a weekend: the diamond district was almost empty, and the theft was only discovered on Monday morning.

Les voleurs ont donc relié deux choses : le jour choisi, et la vigilance autour du bâtiment. Ils pensaient que, quand l’une change, l’autre change aussi. C’est exactement la question de ce chapitre : comment mesurer, avec des nombres, que deux grandeurs bougent ensemble ?

So the thieves linked two things: the chosen day, and the vigilance around the building. They believed that when one changes, the other changes too. That is exactly the question of this chapter: how do you measure, with numbers, that two quantities move together?

Prenons un petit relevé d’exemple, du type qu’un préparateur aurait pu faire. L’idée est simple : peu de gens aiment travailler sous la pluie. Les agents restent-ils à l’abri quand il pleut ?

Comment relier les deux écarts (𝑥𝑖 − ̄𝑥) et (𝑦𝑖 − ̄𝑦) d’un même jour ? Il y a quatre opérations simples. Prends le jour 3 : écart de pluie +4,8, écart d’agents −2,6. L’addition donne 2,2 et la soustraction 7,4 : un grand écart de pluie cache le petit écart d’agents, on ne voit plus s’ils vont dans le même sens. La division donne −1,85 : elle dit combien de fois un écart est plus grand que l’autre, pas s’ils montent ou descendent ensemble. Reste la multiplication : (+4,8) × (−2,6) = −12,48. Le produit de deux nombres est positif quand ils ont le même signe, négatif sinon. C’est exactement l’information cherchée.

How do we link the two deviations (𝑥𝑖 − ̄𝑥) and (𝑦𝑖 − ̄𝑦) of the same day? There are four simple operations. Take day 3 rain deviation +4.8, agent deviation −2.6. Addition gives 2.2 and subtraction 7.4: a large rain deviation hides the small agent deviation, you can no longer see whether they go the same way. Division gives −1.85: it says how many times one deviation is larger than the other, not whether they rise or fall together. Multiplication remains: (+4.8) × (−2.6) = −12.48. The product of two numbers is positive when they have the same sign, negative otherwise. That is exactly the information we want.

Covariance is the mean (with 𝑛 − 1) of the signed areas of the rectangles running from the mean point 𝐺 to each point: the product of two deviations is positive when both variables move away from their means on the same side.

Why bother turning it into a matrix? Because a matrix is an object of linear algebra, and all its tools then apply to data. A symmetric matrix has real eigenvalues and perpendicular eigenvectors (chapter 35). For a covariance matrix these eigenvalues are also positive or zero. It is called positive semi-definite; it is positive definite, with strictly positive eigenvalues, when no variable is an exact combination of the others. The geometric reason is shown in chapter 41: each eigenvalue is a variance, and a variance cannot be negative.
