Does Newton always win? In number of iterations, often. In time, not necessarily. A Newton iteration requires computing the 𝑛2 entries of the Hessian, then solving the system 𝐻𝑑 = −∇𝑓 . With our numbers: with 𝑛 = 1 000 parameters, the Hessian has 106 entries, and each iteration requires about a billion operations. A descent iteration, on the other hand, only needs the 𝑛 components of the gradient. So fewer iterations do not mean less computation: one Newton iteration can cost far more than thousands of descent steps.
