The bee covered one single distance. Which of the three is right? None. And you have no reference value to know how far off each one is. You only see, on the drawings of chapter 55, that 12 rectangles leave less blue uncovered than 4. But “better” is not “right”.

Ce problème a longtemps gêné les mathématiciens. Depuis Archimède, on savait approcher une aire courbe. Mais on ne savait pas dire précisément quand une suite d’approximations définit une aire. En 1854, le mathématicien allemand Bernhard Riemann a donné la réponse qui sert encore aujourd’hui. On fait grandir le nombre de rectangles sans fin, et on regarde vers quel nombre les sommes se dirigent. Les sommes de rectangles portent depuis son nom.

This problem bothered mathematicians for a long time. Since Archimedes, people knew how to approximate a curved area. But they could not say precisely when a sequence of approximations defines one area. In 1854 the German mathematician Bernhard Riemann gave the answer still used today. Let the number of rectangles grow without end, and look at which number the sums head towards. Sums of rectangles have carried his name ever since.
